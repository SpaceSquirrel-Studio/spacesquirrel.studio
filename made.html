How Kneady Controls was made

AI use is controversial. As an artist it intimidates me. However, I'm also blown away by how much I can accomplish with it.

I love making 3D tools and developing products. Agentic coding and AI assistance have enabled me to make the tools I never thought I'd get to make again, and that's exciting. One thing matters above all: I believe that if you use AI to make something, you should say how. So here's how Kneady Controls was made.

What I did: The concept, design and interaction model are mine, drawn from years designing and managing 3D tools. I directed and tested every feature through seemingly endless iterations until satisfied. I also built the demo controls and recorded the voiceover.

What AI did: All of Kneady's code was written by Claude Code, working from my designs and direction. I use Claude across the rest of Tangentsmith too: building the website and docs, shaping the logos with me, drafting social posts that I review before they go out, and as a sounding board for planning.

Demo assets: Rain is by Blender Studio. The sneaker and fire truck are from creators on Superhive, credited in the video description.

What that means for you: A person designed Kneady, tested it, and stands behind it. If something breaks, you reach me, an artist who loves making stuff.

Greg Brown, Tangentsmith
